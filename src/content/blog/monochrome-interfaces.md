Color is information, and interfaces are full of it — but most of that
information is noise. Red badges, blue links, green toasts: a whole grammar of
urgency that mostly says "look at me."

A monochrome interface says: *the content is the signal.* Ink on paper has
carried serious reading for centuries. E-readers figured out that you do not
need a backlight to read — you need contrast and calm.

## Rules I try to follow

- **One background, one ink.** Paper and ink, with maybe a softer ink for
  secondary text.
- **Color only for meaning.** If a link is the only blue thing on the page,
  blue means "link." If everything is colored, nothing is.
- **Texture over decoration.** A faint paper grain does more for warmth than
  a gradient ever did.

The entrance to this site is monochrome ASCII for exactly this reason: a
wall of black-and-white characters can be dramatic without a single hue. And
the pages inside are set like an e-reader, because reading is the point.

> Constraint is not the enemy of beauty; it is usually where beauty starts.

Try it: pick one page you use daily and remove every color but ink, paper,
and one accent. You will probably keep it.
